It is easy to collect course bookmarks and still feel unsure what to learn next. A useful learning plan starts with a problem or direction, not with a long list of popular subjects. In 90 days, you can build a foundation in one focused area, practise the concepts and produce a small piece of evidence. That does not guarantee a new job or a promotion, but it gives your learning a purpose.

This guide offers a practical structure for choosing from SkillNorth's current Alison course selection and turning study into action.

## Before you begin: choose one learning outcome

Write a sentence that describes what you want to be able to do. Examples include: “I want to prepare and explain a clean spreadsheet report,” “I want to understand basic cybersecurity risks,” or “I want to plan a small project with clear scope and responsibilities.” Avoid goals such as “learn everything about AI” because they are too broad to guide a 90-day plan.

Then identify the context: your current role, a possible career direction, a personal project or a gap you have noticed. The more specific the context, the easier it is to decide which course and practice activity are relevant.

## Days 1–30: explore and build foundations

**Week 1 — Define the skill.** Read a relevant SkillNorth cluster guide and identify the core concepts. Note what you already know and what is new.

**Week 2 — Choose one course.** Review the syllabus, level, estimated study time, language, course type and any prerequisites on the live provider page. Choose a course that fits your starting point. Do not choose only because its title sounds impressive.

**Weeks 3–4 — Study actively.** Take notes in your own words. After each section, write one question the material helps you answer. If the course includes assessments, use mistakes to identify what needs another review.

At the end of day 30, write a short summary of what you understand and what remains unclear.

## Days 31–60: practise the skill

Choose one small task that uses the concepts you have studied. Keep it realistic and safe. For data analysis, prepare a small public or fictional dataset. For project management, create a project brief and risk list. For digital marketing, write a campaign plan for a clearly defined audience. For programming, build a small script using sample information. For HR or leadership, draft a fictional process or conversation plan.

Record your steps and the decisions you made. Ask someone to review the result if appropriate. Feedback helps reveal whether you can explain the work, not just complete it privately.

## Days 61–90: demonstrate and decide what comes next

Improve the practice project using the feedback. Write a short case note with the goal, process, tools or concepts used, result and limitations. Keep examples free of confidential information and personal data. If you share the work publicly, make sure you own or have permission to share every asset and dataset.

At the end of 90 days, decide whether to deepen the same skill, add a complementary one or choose a different direction. The decision should be based on what you enjoyed, what you could demonstrate and what the roles or projects you care about actually require.

## A simple weekly schedule

A plan should fit around work, family and other responsibilities. One possible rhythm is three study sessions of 30–45 minutes and one longer practice session each week. Use the sessions for learning, retrieval practice and application—not just passive watching. Adjust the schedule to your needs; consistency is more useful than an unrealistic timetable.

## How to know whether the plan is working

Review progress every two weeks. Ask: Can I explain the concepts without reading my notes? Can I apply them to a new example? Can I identify mistakes or limitations? Can I show a small result to another person? If the answer is no, adjust the plan rather than simply adding more courses.

## Your 90-day commitment

Choose one capability. Select one suitable learning resource. Practise the concepts in a small project. Document what you can do and what you still need to learn. A focused 90-day plan will not answer every career question, but it can turn uncertainty into a concrete next step.

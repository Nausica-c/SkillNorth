Digital marketing is not simply posting on social media or buying online ads. It is the work of understanding an audience, defining a useful offer, choosing channels, communicating clearly and measuring whether an activity supports a goal.

In 2027, marketers also need to understand how AI tools can support research, drafting and analysis without replacing judgement. Tools change quickly; strategy, customer understanding and measurement remain essential foundations.

## Start with the customer and the goal

Before choosing a channel, identify who you want to reach and what they need. A useful audience description goes beyond age or location: it considers the problem a person is trying to solve, what they already know, what may stop them from acting and which information would help them decide.

Then define the goal. Awareness, lead generation, sales and customer retention are different objectives. Each needs a suitable message and a way to measure progress.

## Five digital marketing skills to build

### 1. Understand the customer journey

Learn how awareness, consideration and action differ. A person discovering a brand for the first time needs different information from someone comparing alternatives or returning for support. Map the questions that arise at each stage.

### 2. Plan content and channel roles

Content should answer a real audience question. Search, email, social media and landing pages can play different roles in a campaign. Choose a channel because it fits the audience and the goal, not because it is currently fashionable.

### 3. Write clear messages and landing pages

Explain what is offered, who it helps and what the next step involves. Avoid exaggerated claims and make important conditions visible. A clear landing page helps people decide whether an offer is relevant.

### 4. Measure meaningful outcomes

Choose a small number of useful metrics. Reach and impressions can help describe exposure, but they do not automatically show business impact. Depending on the goal, you may need to understand visits, engagement, qualified leads, conversion rates or repeat activity. Keep track of the time period and the source of the data.

### 5. Use AI responsibly

AI tools can support brainstorming, summarising research, drafting variations and organising information. They can also generate inaccurate claims or generic content. Check facts, protect personal and confidential information, respect copyright and make sure a human reviews important customer-facing messages.

## A small campaign exercise

Choose one audience and one objective. Write a short audience description, a clear message and a simple landing-page outline. Decide which channel would be the best first test and select one primary metric. After the test, write down what happened, what you can reasonably conclude and what you still do not know.

A small, well-documented exercise can teach more than publishing a large volume of content without a clear purpose.

## Common questions

### Do I need to know how to code?

Not for many entry-level marketing tasks. Basic familiarity with analytics, websites and digital tools is useful, while technical roles may require coding, tagging or data skills.

### Is AI replacing digital marketers?

AI can change tasks and workflows, but marketing still requires audience understanding, positioning, creative judgement, collaboration and accountability. The practical goal is to learn how to evaluate and use tools appropriately.

### Which digital marketing skill should I learn first?

Start with audience research and goal-setting. These help you decide which content, channel and metric make sense. Then deepen your skills in SEO, email, social media or analytics according to your direction.

## What to learn in this cluster

Effective teaching begins with a clear learning outcome. Educators then choose an explanation, activity and assessment that help learners move towards that outcome. Educational psychology offers ways to think about attention, memory, motivation, development and differences between learners.

The selected Alison courses introduce educational psychology and teaching principles. Turn the ideas into practice by designing a short lesson plan, including a learning objective, an activity and a way to check understanding.

## Who this is for

This cluster is for people exploring teaching and training, current educators seeking additional learning and professionals who design or deliver workplace instruction.

## Skills to practise beyond the course

Teaching and training start with the learner and the intended outcome. Practise writing objectives that describe what a learner should be able to do, choosing an activity that supports that outcome and checking understanding in a fair way. Educational psychology introduces ideas about attention, memory, motivation, development and learner differences. These ideas are most useful when they influence practical decisions about explanation, practice and feedback.

For workplace training, consider how learners will use the skill after the session. A short demonstration, guided practice and a realistic scenario may be more useful than a long slide deck. For formal teaching roles, requirements vary by education level and country; a short course does not automatically provide teacher licensure or replace supervised classroom experience.

## A practical way to apply what you learn

Design a 15-minute mini-lesson. Write one measurable learning objective, plan a short explanation, add an activity where learners use the idea and create one question or task that checks understanding. After a test run, note where learners hesitated and what you would change. This gives you a concrete example of instructional thinking to develop further.

## What to learn in this cluster

Accounting provides a structured way to record and interpret financial activity. The core statements answer different questions: an income statement summarises performance over a period, a balance sheet shows a position at a point in time, and a cash-flow statement tracks movements of cash.

The featured course introduces foundational accounting concepts and the accounting equation. Practise by reading a simple set of statements and explaining the difference between profit and cash. That distinction helps prevent common misunderstandings.

## Who this is for

This hub is aimed at beginners, founders, administrators, team leads and non-finance professionals who want to read business numbers with greater confidence.

## Skills to practise beyond the course

Financial literacy begins with understanding the story behind the numbers. Learn how transactions are recorded, why accounts are grouped and how the main financial statements relate to one another. Revenue is not the same as profit; profit is not the same as cash; and a balance sheet describes a position at a point in time rather than performance over a whole year. These distinctions matter in everyday business decisions.

For a next step, explore budgeting, bookkeeping workflows, cost concepts and basic financial analysis. The level of depth required depends on whether you want to manage a personal budget, support a small business, work in administration or pursue an accounting career. Professional accounting and tax work can require formal education, supervised experience and local registration or licensing.

## A practical way to apply what you learn

Use a fictional small-business example. Categorise a short list of transactions, prepare a simple income-and-expense summary and compare the resulting profit with the cash position. Explain what the figures do not tell you. Keep the exercise educational and use fictional or properly anonymised information rather than uploading private financial records to public tools.

## What to learn in this cluster

Data analysis begins with reliable inputs. Before creating dashboards, learn how rows, columns, formulas, data types and missing values affect a result. Spreadsheet skills are useful because many teams still exchange information through workbooks and simple reports.

The Alison course listed here is a focused Excel starting point. Use it to practise formulas and data cleaning, then extend your learning into SQL, statistics or a visualisation platform when your goal requires them.

## Who this is for

This cluster is relevant to people in operations, administration, sales, finance, marketing and project support who want to make better use of the information already available in their work.

## Skills to practise beyond the course

Strong data work starts before the chart. Check where a dataset came from, what each row represents, whether units are consistent and how blanks or duplicates are handled. In Excel, practise formulas, sorting, filtering, tables and clear naming. Learn to distinguish a calculated result from an assumption, and keep a simple record of cleaning decisions so another person can follow your work.

As your needs grow, consider SQL for querying structured data, statistics for interpreting patterns and a visualisation tool for communicating results. You do not need to learn every tool at once. Choose the next one based on the kind of question you want to answer and the systems used in your workplace or target role.

## A practical way to apply what you learn

Use a small public dataset or a sample workbook. Clean the data, explain three decisions you made, calculate two useful measures and create one chart that answers a specific question. Finish with a short note that states the finding, the evidence and one limitation. A transparent, well-explained workbook is a better first portfolio item than a decorative dashboard with unclear assumptions.
